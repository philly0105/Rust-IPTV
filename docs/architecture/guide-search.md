# Guide programme search

Code: `crates/iptv-core/src/epg/search.rs` (core), migrations `crates/iptv-core/src/db/migrations/0015_guide.sql`, `0019_guide_search_speed.sql`, `0027_guide_catalog_stamp.sql`, `0028_guide_search_order.sql` and `0029_guide_search_sets.sql`. Benchmark of the index's upkeep: `crates/iptv-core/tests/guide_rebuild_bench.rs` (ignored; rebuild, startup build, refreshes and a new catalog on a 885k-programme guide and 200k channels, with a favourite toggled every 20 ms meanwhile). Tests: `crates/iptv-core/tests/guide_search.rs` (matching, window, limit, hidden and locked channels, the scan before the index exists, scan and index parity on Greek and Vietnamese titles, index upkeep, query plans, timing on 200k programmes and a 50k-channel source), `crates/iptv-core/tests/l3_search_epg.rs` (two large sources on one guide: hits against an independent model and the scan, query plans, release p95) and unit tests in `search.rs` and `tmdb/normalize.rs` (the diacritic fold).

## Command

`epg.search {sourceId, query, from?, to?, limit?}` returns `GuideSearchHit[]`:

```
{ ref, channelName, channelNumber?, logoUrl?, categoryId?, program: EpgProgram }
```

- `query`: 2 to 100 characters after trimming. Every word of the query (a run of letters and digits, lowercased, diacritics removed, final sigma read as sigma) must start a word of the title, so `cafe soc` finds "Café Society" and "CAFE SOCIETY" but `afe` finds nothing. Diacritics are removed as Unicode NFD does (`tmdb::normalize::strip_diacritics`, a table generated from the Unicode decomposition data), so `ελληνικα` finds "Ελληνικά" and `thoi su` finds "Thời sự".
- `from` and `to`: RFC 3339. `from` defaults to now, `to` to `from` + 14 days; `to` must be after `from` and at most 16 days later. A programme matches when it overlaps the window (`stop > from` and `start < to`), so the one on air at `from` counts.
- `limit`: 1 to 200, default 50.
- `program.start` and `program.end` are guide times moved by the hit channel's guide shift (its own shift or `tvg-shift`, plus the source's; `query::item_shift`, read once per channel, epg.md), as `epg.programs` and `epg.grid` return them, so a hit shows the same time as the grid cell it opens. The UI applies `epgOffsetMinutes`.
- The window applies to those shifted times. The source's shift moves every channel of the source alike, so the window is read that much earlier on the guide's clock (`candidates`), and the order holds. A channel's own shift or `tvg-shift` differs per channel. `candidates` reads it with the channel as it takes the hits in start and channel-number order, and skips a hit it moves out of the window before the limit counts it; when that leaves fewer than `limit`, it walks on to later starts until the limit is met or the hits run out. Only the skipped channels are read beyond the ones the result needs, so a source without channel shifts costs what it did before. Known gap: the walk and the limit go by the start moved by the source's shift only, so a programme that only the channel's own part moves into the window is not found, and one it moves ahead of others may lose its place to the limit. A full fix needs every channel's shift before the walk (the `tvg-shift` lives in `items.extra_json`, outside the covering index `idx_items_guide`; reading it for 44,000 channels took about 20 ms of SQLite time on an Intel N150), the raw window widened by the source's spread of channel shifts, and the hits cut and sorted by shifted start, checked against `l3_search_epg.rs`'s budget.
- Errors: `invalid` for bad arguments (including an empty `sourceId`), `not_found` for an unknown source.

## Which channels a hit belongs to

Hits are mapped to the live and radio channels of the requested source only. A guide channel maps to an item the way the grid maps it, minus fuzzy matching:

1. A manual mapping in `epg_channel_map` wins. A mapping to NULL means "no guide" and the item gets no hits.
2. Otherwise the item's `epg_channel_id` (tvg-id), compared case-insensitively against the enabled feeds.
3. Otherwise the exact normalised channel name. Similar names ("Channel Threee") never match here, so search does not show programmes from a guessed channel.

A guide channel can map to several items (an HD and an SD copy); each gets its own hit. When two feeds carry the same channel and start, the feed added first wins.

Left out:
- channels hidden with channel edits (`channeledit::scoped` in the channel query); a renamed channel shows its custom name in `channelName`;
- parental-locked channels and channels in locked or adult categories while hide mode is on (`parental::filter::scoped` in the same query). They never become candidates, so any number of hits on locked channels cannot push a visible hit past the limit;
- while the Live TV quality filter is on (`liveMinQuality`), live channels whose name marks a lower quality, and with `liveHideUntagged` those with no quality tag (the quality clause in the same query, catalog.md). Radio channels stay;
- disabled feeds and the staging rows of a refresh in progress.

Order: programme start, then channel number (channels without one last), then channel name, then item id. Because a shift can move a hit past others, the hits are sorted again by their shifted start (a stable sort, so equal starts keep the channel order).

## The index

`epg_programs_fts` is an external-content FTS5 table over `epg_programs.title` (`tokenize='unicode61 remove_diacritics 2'`, `columnsize=0`). It is not kept in step row by row: a refresh writes its programmes through staging and swaps them in with a few bulk statements, and triggers would add their cost to every one of those rows. Instead the whole index is rebuilt (`rebuild_index`): one sort of every programme of a configured feed into a temporary table in search order, (start, feed rowid, programme rowid), with its title folded on the way, from which `epg_search_rows` and the index are filled in that order. The temporary table's rowids (1, 2, ...) become the ids of both, so the index's rowids are not programme rowids (migration 0028): the index is only ever asked for `MATCH` and its rowids, never for column values, ranks or column sizes, so its content setting is never read and it keeps no sizes (`columnsize=0`, migration 0029). FTS5 takes rows fastest in ascending rowid order, which is why the titles are sorted with the rest instead of looked up by programme rowid afterwards (that took twice as long).

There are two sets of these tables (migration 0029, `IndexSet`): `epg_programs_fts` with `epg_search_rows`, and `epg_programs_fts_b` with `epg_search_rows_b`. The marker names the current one; a rebuild fills the other, so searches go on reading the current set (or scanning, when a change removed the marker) while it runs:

1. In a read transaction, it reads the guide stamp (below) and sorts the programmes into the temporary table. That writes only the temporary database, so it holds no write lock. The sort always goes through a temporary file (`temp_store=FILE` for the build, restored after), behind the temporary database's small cache.
2. It copies the sorted rows into the other set in write transactions of about 25 ms each (`CHUNK_TARGET`; the first copies 8,192 rows, each next one is sized from the last, at most twice as many). Between two of them it hands the write lock over: while a build runs, busy waits poll every 2 ms at most (`db::handing_over`), and when another connection waited during a transaction the build pauses 4 ms before the next (`db::hand_over`), because SQLite's lock has no queue and a writer that begins again at once would keep it from waiters that sleep between polls. The first transaction empties the set when it holds rows.
3. The last transaction also sets the longest programme's length and the marker to the new set. Each transaction first checks the guide stamp: when it moved (a refresh committed its swap, the guide was cleared or pruned), the build drops what it did and starts again from step 1 with a new sort, up to three times (`BUILD_ATTEMPTS`); after that it gives up with the marker unset (searches scan), and the change's own rebuild follows.
4. A last short transaction empties the set read before, so the file does not hold two indexes and the next build fills an empty set.

One build runs at a time per database file (`build_lock`). `ensure_index` waits for a running build and then does nothing when it left the index current: of two refreshes that commit back to back, the first one's build sees the second swap at its next transaction and starts again, and the second one's rebuild finds the index current. A build that fails part-way (interrupted, the app shut down) leaves the current set and the marker as they were: the other set holds a partial copy that no search reads and the next build empties; the temporary table is dropped on the way out, also on errors. When no set is current, a build fills an empty set if there is one.

On the benchmark's guide (885k programmes, 200k channels, `guide_rebuild_bench.rs`, release, 2026-11-13, three alternating rounds of three rebuilds on a 4-core machine shared with two browser test runs) a rebuild took 1.66-2.15 s (median 1.82 s) and 1.56-1.83 s of CPU (median 1.74 s) in one transaction before these changes, and a favourite toggled every 20 ms meanwhile waited 1.63-1.93 s for it; peak memory rose by up to 83 MB (the startup, clear and prune rebuilds sorted in memory). Now: 1.50-1.82 s (median 1.65 s) and 1.28-1.49 s of CPU (median 1.37 s), toggles wait 18-47 ms at most (once 111 ms), and peak memory rises 32 MB (once 64 MB). After a refresh of the 24k-programme feed, which rebuilds the whole index, a toggle waited 1.8-2.25 s before and 131-153 ms after (the rest is the swap). Of the CPU, the sort takes about 0.65 s (folding the titles about 0.05 s of it), the search rows 0.26 s and the title index 0.2 s (0.47 s with column sizes).

The index holds titles folded by the same Rust function the scan and the query use (`search::fold`, registered as the SQL function `epg_search_fold` for the rebuild), not the raw titles a `'rebuild'` would read from the content table. The tokenizer's own diacritic removal covers Latin letters only and keeps the Greek tonos, so with raw titles a Greek query found nothing through the index while the scan found it. Query terms are folded before they reach the tokenizer, so both sides of a match pass through the same fold and then the same tokenizer.

`epg_search_rows` (migration 0019, keyed anew by 0028; `epg_search_rows_b` in the second set) holds one narrow row per programme, numbered in search order (`id`): `prog` (the programme rowid), `start`, `stop`, `feed` (the `epg_sources` rowid of the programme's feed) and `channel_id` (the guide channel). These are the columns the index path filters and orders each hit by, so a hit does not read the programme's wide row with its description. A set's rows and title index are filled from the same sort and made current by one marker write, so the two always describe the same programmes. Since the ids follow start order, the first row starting at or after a time is a binary search over ids (`search_rows_from`, about 20 rowid probes). The `app_state` key `epg_search_longest`, set by the same transaction, holds the longest programme's length in seconds.

The `app_state` key `epg_search_index` says whether a set of index tables matches the table, and which: `ordered-1` for the first set, `ordered-1b` for the second (any other value counts as missing, so an older build that meets `ordered-1b` rebuilds):

- Every statement that deletes or swaps programmes removes the key in the same transaction (`invalidate_index`): `store::commit_staging`, `clear_all`, `prune`, and the staging cleanups when they deleted rows. The same call writes a new random value under `epg_search_guide`, the guide stamp a build checks before each of its transactions.
- `rebuild_index` fills a set and sets the key, as above. It runs after each refresh (while the import's larger page cache is still set), after `epg.clearAll`, after a prune, and once in the background at startup when the key is missing or holds another value and programmes exist (a database upgraded from before migration 15, an index marked `ready` (raw titles), `folded-1` (no search rows) or `folded-2` (keyed by programme rowid) by an earlier build, an upgrade to 0029, or a crash between a swap and its rebuild).
- Migrations 0015, 0019, 0028 and 0029 leave the index tables empty and remove the key (0029 creates the first set's title index again without column sizes and empties its search rows: 19-32 ms in all over an 885k-row index, timed with Python's `sqlite3` on a copy of the tables) so the upgrade stays fast; the startup build fills one set.

The startup build runs on a pooled connection in a background task (`spawn_startup_build`); nothing at startup waits for it. Until it is done, searches scan (results the same, but 1.3-1.7 s each on the benchmark's 200k channels and 885k programmes in release), reads go on as before (WAL), and writes wait at most one of its transactions: a favourite toggled every 20 ms during a startup build (with a search every 100 ms) waited 2.9-6.1 s at most before 2026-11-13 and 85-182 ms after (the build, searches included, took 3.4-7.0 s of wall time before and 2.7-4.1 s after; three alternating rounds). Migration 0019 does rebuild five `items` indexes once (`idx_items_added_src`, `idx_items_title`, `idx_items_pos`, `idx_items_rating`, `idx_items_year`, catalog.md), about 2.4 s per 220k items, while the database opens.

Search reads a set of index tables only while the key names it, and reads the key in the same read transaction as the set, so a build that makes the other set current meanwhile does not change what it reads. Otherwise it scans the time window (`idx_epg_programs_stop`) and matches titles in Rust with the same word rule. Results are the same either way, only slower (about 1 s for 200k programmes in a debug build), and never stale: a rowid reused by a later insert cannot point a hit at the wrong programme, because the key is gone until the next rebuild. A search reads its hits and their programme details in one read transaction, so a refresh that commits in between cannot hand a hit's rowid to another programme.

The index statement (`FTS_SQL`) returns the hits with their `epg_search_rows` row in id order, which is (start, feed) order, without a sort: from the first row that starts the longest programme's length before the window (any programme still on air at the window's start starts after that), up to the first row that starts at or after the window's end, keeping those that end after its start. `candidates` reads them in batches (512 hits, then twice as many each time, with `LIMIT` and the last id read as the next lower bound), drops hits of disabled or staging feeds, and reads the source's channels of each guide channel as it first shows up (`ChannelFinder`, below); hits of guide channels the source does not have are dropped. The scan sorts its hits by start and feed. Both then walk the hits up to the start time at which `limit` channel slots are reached (every channel of a guide channel counts; a slot two feeds list counts once), so ties are ordered the same either way; the index path reads another batch whenever the hits read so far end before the walk has settled (no hit yet past the cut, or a channel shift moved too many out and the walk goes on). On the benchmark's guide, `news` matches 108k programmes, 84k of them in the default 14-day window; a search used to read all 84k search rows and sort them to keep 50, and now reads the first batch or two. Only the channels that can make the first `limit` hits are then read in full (item id, name, logo, category, guide shift), and only the final hits read their programme row (title, description, category, icon).

## Speed

`guide_search.rs` searches 1,000 guide channels with 200 programmes each (200k) from a source of 50,000 channels, 49,000 of them with guide ids and names the guide does not know. A common word (`news`, thousands of matches), a rare title and two-word queries each answered in about 35 to 60 ms when the test was written, 7.7-14.7 ms on 2026-11-10 before that day's two changes (reading only the channels of guide channels with hits, then the hits in start order) and 0.2-3.2 ms after them (one release run each on a busy machine; the index build went from 216 to 315 ms); the test asserts under 150 ms in release builds.

`l3_search_epg.rs` searches two sources that share one guide of 440 channels with 240 hourly programmes each (105,600), plus a second feed repeating some slots and a disabled feed: an M3U source of 44,000 live channels on 400 guide channels and 1,000 radio channels the guide does not know, and an Xtream-like source of 20,000 live channels on 40 guide ids. `news` matches about 15,800 programmes and `the` about 26,400. The budget is a p95 under 50 ms in release; the test asserts 75 ms to leave room for a machine loaded by other builds (300 ms in debug), and a query without hits under 5 ms (40 ms in debug). On 2026-11-10 its p50 went from 10.2-20.7 ms to 7.3-12.9 ms across the four cases with those two changes (one release run each; both sources map most of their channels to the guide, so the search walks them rather than reading by key). `l2_query_epg_search.rs` (45,000 live channels on 5,000 guide channels): `news` 18.5 to 11.3 ms, `zebra cross` 14.6 to 0.8 ms at p50.

### The channels of the guide channels with hits

On the index path the hits come first, and only the channels of the guide channels among them are read (`ChannelFinder` in `search.rs`, one call per batch of hits with the guide channels new in it). What a channel resolves to depends on its (`epg_channel_id`, title) pair and the guide alone, so the search keeps, per source and channel type, every distinct pair of the catalog (`CatalogKeys`, on the EPG runtime): sorted the way `idx_items_guide` holds them, with the hash of each pair's trimmed, lowercased id and of its normalized title (`normalize_name`) in two sorted lists. For each guide channel with hits it looks up, the other way round from resolving a channel: the lowercased ids the channel directory turns into that channel (`ChannelDirectory::ids_of`), the channel's own id when the directory does not know it (it has programmes, since it has hits; the probe of the forward path), and the normalized names that match it exactly (`names_of`), keeping a name match only for a pair whose id resolves to nothing (that check probes `epg_programs` for an unknown id, remembered per guide generation). Each candidate is checked against its own text, so a hash collision costs a comparison and nothing else. Manual mappings (`epg_channel_map`) are read on every search and win as on the forward path; a mapping to NULL leaves the channel out.

The pairs' channels are then read in one statement (`SEEK_SQL`): the pairs go in as a JSON array (`json_each`), and each is an index-only seek on `idx_items_guide` by (`source_id`, `content_type`, `epg_channel_id IS`, `title =`), with the same visibility clauses as the channel walk (channel edits, hide mode, the quality filter), so those apply at once; channels mapped by hand are read by rowid (`MAPPED_SQL`). When the channels asked for by key in a search would come to more than a quarter of the source's (`SEEK_COST`), one walk of the source's visible channels is cheaper: it is taken once, with each row's pair looked up in the sorted list as the scan goes, and kept by pair for the rest of the search. Guide channels without a visible channel drop out with their hits.

The list is complete only for the catalog it was read from. Migration 0027 adds `guide_catalog_stamp(source_id, content_type, stamp)`, a random number that every write which adds, removes or re-keys a live or radio row replaces in the same transaction: `CatalogWriter::finish` for the types it rewrote (every importer; its per-row triggers are suspended), `sources::store::delete` (it removes the row), the `items_ai` and `items_ad` triggers for any other insert or delete (their FTS statement and the stamp share the suspend check, so a suspended bulk write pays nothing per row), and `items_guide_au` for an update of `source_id`, `content_type`, `epg_channel_id` or `title`. A search reads the stamp inside its read transaction and loads the list again when it differs, carrying the name hashes of the pairs it already had (normalizing every title is most of a load). The guide's side needs no invalidation of its own: the lists hold no resolutions, and the channel directory and the programme probes follow the runtime generation as before. Events and their tests (`guide_search.rs`, `follows_catalog_and_guide_changes_*`, run once reading by key and once walking the source, each result also compared with the scan that resolves every channel afresh): a catalog rewrite through the writer, rows inserted, re-keyed, renamed and deleted outside it, manual mappings set and cleared, a guide refresh that renames a channel and brings programmes for an id no `<channel>` declares, a feed disabled and enabled, and the source removed and added again; hidden and renamed channels and hide mode in `hidden_renamed_and_locked_channels` and `locked_channels_do_not_starve_visible_hits`, the quality filter in `quality_user_views.rs`. Lists past 1,000,000 pairs in all (`KEYS_CAP`, about 60 bytes each) drop the other sources' lists.

On the 200k-channel benchmark (release, `crates/iptv-core/tests/read_rpc_bench.rs`, 2026-11-10, three alternating rounds on a 4-core machine busy with other builds and browsers, so only large differences count): reading only the channels of the guide channels with hits took the first `news` search after the guide changed (`BENCH_COLD=1`) from 445-744 ms to 145-172 ms at p50 and a warm `news` from 98-200 ms to 85-124 ms, of which the channels took about 19 ms (3,000 seeks) and the FTS statement with its 84k search rows about 52 ms. Reading the hits in start order and stopping once settled then took them to 17-18 ms (cold) and 5.7-6.3 ms (warm), against 422-426 ms and 99-111 ms on the base in the same three alternating rounds (a quieter machine; every other benchmark case within noise). What is left of the cold search is mostly the channel directory's reload, which is now loaded ahead (below).

### Loaded ahead

What the first search after a change would load is loaded in the background as the change completes, without holding any lock a read needs (a search that comes meanwhile waits for the directory's load instead of starting its own):

- the channel directory with its reverse lookups (`spawn_warm_guide`), after a refresh that stored data, `epg.clearAll`, a prune and `epg.setEnabled`;
- a source's catalog keys (`spawn_warm_catalog`, live and radio, read in one read transaction as a search reads them), after an import that wrote its catalog (`sources::run_import_inner`, spawned once the import has committed and reported done, so the import never waits for it);
- at startup, after the startup build (or at once when the index is current), the directory and every source's catalog keys.

On the benchmark (`guide_rebuild_bench.rs`, release, 2026-11-13, three alternating rounds; the search 1.5 s after the refresh returned): the first `news` search after refreshing a feed took 23-50 ms before and 8.9-22 ms after (the next one 6-15 ms either way). The first search on a new 200k-channel catalog, 3 s after its import, took 121-180 ms before (reading and normalizing every channel name, about 110 ms of it) and 11-38 ms after, against 7-12 ms for the next one. The keys take about 60 bytes per distinct channel key in memory from the import or startup on, whether or not the source is searched (about 12 MB for 200k).

The scan (no index yet) still reads every channel of the source first, as below, and so did the index path until 2026-11. The channel statement is an index-only scan of `idx_items_guide` (migration 0019, `quality` appended by 0022), a partial index on `items(source_id, content_type, epg_channel_id, title, number, category_id, quality)` over live and radio rows; the statement names `content_type IN ('live', 'radio')` literally so the planner can use it, `category_id` is there for hide mode's category filter, and `quality` for the Live TV quality filter. On the index path a query without hits does not read the channels. For the scan, what depends only on the guide is memoized on the EPG runtime (`SearchMemo`) until its generation moves (a refresh, a clear, a prune, a feed enabled or disabled): whether an unknown `epg_channel_id` has programmes (one `epg_programs` probe each) and which guide channel a name matches exactly. Before the memo, every search repeated both for each unmatched channel, about 140 ms at 50k channels. The memo keeps, per source and channel type, each distinct (`epg_channel_id`, title) pair with the guide channel it resolves to, sorted the way the channel statement returns its rows, and the scan walks that list alongside the rows: a channel costs a comparison with the next entries, and a row out of that order or new since the last search falls back to a binary search and then to resolving it afresh, which adds it to the list. Until 2026-11 it was two hash maps (by id, then by title), two lookups in tables of up to the source's size for every channel; at 200k channels on a 3,000-channel, 876k-programme guide, `news` (84k index hits) went from 167-186 ms to 121-128 ms at p50 with a warm memo (release, `crates/iptv-core/tests/read_rpc_bench.rs`, three alternating rounds on an otherwise idle machine); the first search after the guide changed, which resolves every channel afresh, stayed at about 450-530 ms either way. The memo drops its lists past 500,000 entries in all.

## Query plans

`tests/guide_search.rs` checks with `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` that the FTS statement drives from the virtual table index and reads each hit's `epg_search_rows` row by rowid, and that the scan uses `idx_epg_programs_stop`; that the channel seek (`SEEK_SQL`) drives from the JSON list and reads `idx_items_guide` as a covering index on all four key columns, and that `MAPPED_SQL` reads its rows by rowid. `tests/l3_search_epg.rs` checks that the channel statement (`CHANNELS_SQL`) uses `idx_items_guide` as a covering index and that the FTS statement reads `epg_search_rows` by its integer primary key. The FTS and scan statements are also in the `db/mod.rs` index test (after every migration); that test accepts a rowid lookup (`USING INTEGER PRIMARY KEY`) as indexed, which the FTS statement's search-row read is.
